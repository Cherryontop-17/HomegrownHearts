## Part 1 — What grows here, and when

Central NC supports three growing seasons. A bundle contains whatever is actually
ready that week, which is the point — it is not a fixed box.

### Spring (harvested roughly April–June)

| Crop          | Notes                                                  |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Lettuce       | Cut-and-come-again; bolts once nights stay warm        |
| Spinach       | Earliest reliable green                                |
| Kale          | Overwinters; often available well before anything else |
| Chard         | Long season, keeps producing into summer               |
| Radishes      | ~25 days seed to harvest — good for a first delivery   |
| Snap peas     | Short window, very popular                             |
| Beets         | Roots and greens both usable                           |
| Carrots       | Slow; plan the sowing date backwards from a delivery   |
| Spring onions | Reliable filler                                        |
| Turnips       | Roots and greens                                       |

### Summer (harvested roughly June–September)

| Crop          | Notes                                                          |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tomatoes      | The highest-value item in any bundle. Plan around them         |
| Cucumbers     | Heavy producer; pick small and often                           |
| Summer squash | Extremely heavy producer — do not overplant                    |
| Zucchini      | As above. Two plants is plenty                                 |
| Snap beans    | Successive sowings keep it going                               |
| Peppers       | Sweet varieties only (see exclusions)                          |
| Okra          | Thrives in Piedmont heat when little else does                 |
| Herbs         | Basil, parsley, cilantro — small bunches, high perceived value |

### Fall (harvested roughly September–November)

| Crop           | Notes                                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Collard greens | Regionally familiar; improves after frost               |
| Kale           | Second, better crop                                     |
| Broccoli       | Needs an early-August start                             |
| Cabbage        | Stores well, extends the delivery window                |
| Carrots        | Sweeter than the spring crop                            |
| Turnips        | Roots and greens                                        |
| Mustard greens | Fast, regionally familiar                               |
| Winter squash  | Stores for weeks — the only crop that bridges to winter |
| Sweet potatoes | An NC staple. Cure before distributing                  |

## What we deliberately do **not** grow or distribute

This list is as important as the one above, and a clinic will ask.

| Excluded                           | Why                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Raw sprouts** (alfalfa, mung)    | Repeatedly implicated in outbreaks; the growing conditions are the hazard. Never.         |
| **Melons** (cantaloupe especially) | Rind contamination transfers to flesh when cut. Not worth the risk at our scale.          |
| **Unpasteurised anything**         | No juice, no cider, no dairy. We do not process food at all.                              |
| **Hot peppers**                    | Not a safety issue — a handling one. Unlabelled heat in a mixed bundle is a bad surprise. |
| **Foraged or wild items**          | Identification risk. Everything comes from our own beds.                                  |
| **Anything cooked or cut**         | Preparation moves us into a different regulatory category entirely.                       |
| **Home-canned or preserved food**  | Botulism risk. Absolutely never.                                                          |

**We distribute whole, raw, unprocessed produce and nothing else.**

## Part 2 — Food-safety protocol

Based on FDA guidance (S6). This is the section a clinic will actually read, so
it is written as steps a volunteer can follow, not as principles.

### Before harvest

- Anyone handling produce washes hands with soap and warm water for **20 seconds**.
- Nobody who is ill, or who has had vomiting or diarrhoea in the previous 48 hours, handles produce. No exceptions and no judgement about it.
- Harvest containers are washed and fully dried before each use.
- No pets in the beds. No animal manure used as fertiliser at any point in the season.

### At harvest

- Harvest in the **morning**, the same day as delivery, while it is cool.
- Anything touching the ground stays out of the bundle.
- Damaged, bruised, split, or insect-bored items are cut away or discarded. When in doubt, discard — the cost of a discarded cucumber is nothing.
- Produce goes straight into clean containers, out of direct sun.

### Washing

- Rinse under **plain, cool running water**.
- **Never** soap, detergent, dish liquid, vinegar solution, or commercial produce wash. FDA is explicit: these can be absorbed into produce and make people ill. This is the single most common well-meant mistake, so it is stated twice.
- Firm produce (potatoes, carrots, squash) gets a clean produce brush.
- Leafy greens are rinsed leaf by leaf, not as a head.
- Dry with a clean single-use towel or a clean salad spinner. Water left on greens shortens their life sharply.

### Packing

- Clean, dry, food-safe container or a new paper bag. Bags and boxes are never reused between deliveries.
- Heavy items (squash, sweet potatoes) at the bottom, greens and herbs on top.
- Herbs bagged separately — they crush.
- No item is packed that a volunteer would not take home themselves. That is the actual standard.
- Each bundle gets an insert card listing contents, the harvest date, and washing instructions. (The card carries **no** nutrition or health information — Rule 1.)

### Transport and handover

- Delivered **within 4 hours** of packing.
- Cooler with ice packs if the outside temperature is above 75°F.
- Produce never sits in a parked car.
- Handed to clinic staff, who distribute. **Founders never hand a bundle to a recipient**, which is both a safeguarding boundary and the reason the clinic partnership exists.
- Anything undelivered at the end of a run is composted, not held over.

### Recall procedure

If we learn that a delivered bundle may be unsafe, we tell the clinic
**immediately** — same day, by phone, before any other step — with the delivery
date and contents. We do not contact recipients, because we do not and must not
know who they are.

## Open questions for the clinic

Bring this list to the first meeting. Answering these is what converts the
document from ours into theirs.

1. Are there items you want **excluded** for your patient population?
2. Do you have a preferred **container** (bag vs. box), or a storage constraint at your end?
3. What **day and time** suits your distribution schedule?
4. Do you want the insert card in **English, Spanish, or both**? (We can produce both.)
5. Do you need anything from us on paper — a written protocol, a volunteer roster, proof of an adult supervisor?
6. Who is our **single point of contact** if there is ever a problem with a delivery?
