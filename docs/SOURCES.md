# Cited sources

**Owner:** SME role · **Governed by:** [`COMPLIANCE.md`](COMPLIANCE.md) Rule 1

Every number that appears on this site comes from this file, with a link a
clinic staffer can click and check in under a minute. Nothing is cited from
memory, and nothing is cited second-hand where a primary source exists.

---

## The rule that shapes this whole list

**We cite food access and consumption. We never cite health outcomes.**

This is the single most important editorial decision in the project, and it is
easy to get backwards. The obvious statistics for a maternal-nutrition project
are about what nutrition _does_ — birth weight, complications, development. Every
one of those is a health claim under Rule 1, and **a citation does not launder
it**. Attributing a claim to ACOG still means two 15-year-olds published a
statement about pregnancy outcomes.

What we may say instead is entirely factual and, for our actual audience, more
persuasive: fresh produce is expensive, most people do not eat enough of it, and
some families in this county cannot reliably afford food. That is the problem we
address, stated without a word about anyone's body.

## S1 — Vegetable and fruit intake in the U.S.

> **10.0%** of U.S. adults met federal vegetable intake recommendations in 2019.
> **12.3%** met fruit recommendations. Among adults living below or close to the
> poverty level, only **6.8%** met vegetable recommendations.

**Citation:** Lee SH, Moore LV, Park S, Harris DM, Blanck HM. "Adults Meeting
Fruit and Vegetable Intake Recommendations — United States, 2019." _MMWR Morbidity
and Mortality Weekly Report_. 2022;71(1):1–9.
<https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/71/wr/mm7101a1.htm>

**Verified:** figures read from the full text via PubMed Central
([PMC8735562](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8735562/)).

**Why it is safe:** a statement about what people eat, not about what eating does.

**Approved phrasing:**

- "Only about 1 in 10 adults in the U.S. eats the recommended amount of vegetables."
- "That number falls to about 1 in 15 for adults living near the poverty line."

**Do not write:** anything that continues "…which means they miss out on…".

## S2 — Food insecurity in households with children

> **18.4%** of U.S. households with children were food insecure at some point
> during 2024.

**Citation:** Rabbitt MP, et al. _Household Food Security in the United States in
2024_. U.S. Department of Agriculture, Economic Research Service, Report No.
ERR-358. December 2025.
<https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/publications/113623/ERR-358.pdf>

**Why it is safe:** a measure of access, not of outcome.

**Approved phrasing:**

- "Nearly one in five U.S. households with children faced food insecurity in 2024."

## S3 — Food insecurity in Union County, NC

> **11.1%** of people in Union County, NC were food insecure (2023 data,
> published 2025) — among the **lowest** rates in the Carolinas.

**Citation:** Feeding America, _Map the Meal Gap 2025_.
<https://map.feedingamerica.org/>

> ⚠️ **Not yet primary-verified.** This figure came from regional news coverage of
> the study, not from the Feeding America county tool itself, which is an
> interactive application. **Before launch, a founder must open the county tool,
> confirm the number, and record the date checked here.** If it cannot be
> confirmed, delete the number and use S2 instead.

**The honest framing, which matters more than the number.** Union County is a
relatively affluent county, and its food insecurity rate is low compared with its
neighbours. It would be easy — and wrong — to imply we operate in a food desert.
We do not, and a clinic staffer who knows the county would notice immediately and
stop reading.

**Approved phrasing:**

- "Union County is better off than most of the state. Even so, roughly 1 in 9 people here still face food insecurity."

**Do not write:** "Union County families are going hungry," or anything implying
a crisis. The credible version of this argument is the modest one.

## S4 — Nutrition guidance during pregnancy (referral only)

**Citation:** American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists. "Nutrition
During Pregnancy" (patient FAQ).
<https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/nutrition-during-pregnancy>

**This source is never quoted, summarised, or characterised.** It exists so that
a page needing to point somewhere can link out. The only compliant use is a bare
referral:

> For guidance on eating during pregnancy, see ACOG's patient FAQ.

Adding "helpful", "essential reading", or any summary of its contents converts a
referral into a claim we made. See Rule 1.

## S6 — Produce handling and washing

**Citations:**

- U.S. Food and Drug Administration. "Selecting and Serving Produce Safely."
  <https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely>
- U.S. Food and Drug Administration. "7 Tips for Cleaning Fruits, Vegetables."
  <https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/7-tips-cleaning-fruits-vegetables>

Food _safety_ is not a health claim — it is a handling procedure, and a clinic
will expect us to have one. This is the basis for the protocol in
[`PRODUCE.md`](PRODUCE.md). Specific points relied on: wash under plain running
water; **never** use soap, detergent, or commercial produce wash; refrigerate
perishables at 40°F or below; wash hands 20 seconds before and after handling;
cut away damaged areas.

## Adding a source

1. Find the **primary** publication. Not a news article about it, not a blog.
2. Read the actual figure in the actual document.
3. Add an entry here with the full citation, the URL, and the date you checked.
4. Write the approved phrasing, and write the trap next to it.
5. Confirm it is an access or consumption fact. **If it is about a body, it does
   not go on this site**, no matter how good the source is.
