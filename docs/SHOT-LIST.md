# Photography shot list

**For the founders.** Six photographs. One afternoon with a phone, in good
light, and every placeholder on the site is gone.

Where the files go and what they must be named is in [`PHOTOS.md`](PHOTOS.md).
This document is what to actually point the camera at.

---

## Before you start

**The light does almost all the work.** Shoot in the **hour after sunrise or the
hour before sunset**. Midday sun is the single most common reason a garden photo
looks like a snapshot rather than a photograph — it is harsh, it blows out the
greens, and it puts hard shadows across faces.

**Overcast is excellent**, not a setback. Clouds are a giant softbox.

Other things that matter more than the camera:

- **Get low.** Crouch to the height of the plants. Standing and pointing down is the default view and it reads as a phone snap.
- **Get close.** Fill the frame. A wide shot of a whole garden shows a lot of nothing.
- **Clean the frame.** Move the hose, the plastic bag, the empty pot. One coil of hose in the corner is what an admissions reader will notice.
- **Shoot ten of each.** Storage is free. Regret is not.
- **Wipe the lens.** Seriously — a thumbprint costs more sharpness than any setting.

## The six shots

### 1. `hero.jpg` — the wide one · **16:9 landscape**

The image at the top of the homepage. It has to do one job: make a stranger
believe there is a real garden.

- **Subject:** the beds with someone actually working in them — hands in soil, weeding, watering, harvesting.
- **Framing:** landscape, wide, with **space on the left third** for the headline to sit over. Do not centre the subject.
- **Person:** yes, but not looking at the camera. Working. A face is not needed and is better avoided.
- **Time:** golden hour.
- **Avoid:** empty beds, a posed group, anyone smiling at the lens.

> Shoot this one both with and without a person. The version with hands in soil
> will almost certainly win, but have the alternative.

---

### 2. `garden.jpg` — the beds · **4:3**

Proof the growing is real and tended.

- **Subject:** healthy plants at a stage where the crop is identifiable — recognisably lettuce, recognisably tomatoes.
- **Framing:** crouch to plant height. Let a row lead away from the camera.
- **Avoid:** anything wilted or bolted. Photograph the best bed, not the average one.

---

### 3. `harvest.jpg` — just picked · **4:3**

The most naturally beautiful shot on the list, and the easiest to get right.

- **Subject:** a basket or crate of vegetables, **outdoors**, immediately after picking. Soil still on them.
- **Framing:** from slightly above, looking down into the container. Fill the frame.
- **Do:** mix colours — greens next to reds next to purples.
- **Avoid:** a kitchen counter, a supermarket bag, anything washed and shiny. It should look picked, not bought.

---

### 4. `bundle.jpg` — **the credibility shot** · **4:3**

If you only get one right, make it this one. It is what a clinic staffer looks
at when deciding whether we are organised.

- **Subject:** one complete, finished bundle exactly as the clinic receives it — packed bag, contents visible at the top, insert card tucked in.
- **Framing:** straight on or slightly above, plain uncluttered background. A wooden table or clean concrete is ideal.
- **Light:** bright, even, shadowless. Overcast or open shade.
- **Do:** make the bundle look full and deliberately packed.
- **Avoid:** clutter, a messy background, a half-empty bag, a crumpled card.

> ⚠️ **The bundle is photographed alone, before delivery.** Never a bundle being
> handed to anyone, never a recipient, never a hand that is not one of ours.
> [`COMPLIANCE.md`](COMPLIANCE.md) Rule 3.

---

### 5 and 6. `founder-kreeshma.jpg` and `founder-charishya.jpg` · **1:1 square**

Two portraits, shot the same way, so they sit as a pair rather than as two
unrelated pictures.

- **Framing:** head and shoulders, square. Eyes about a third down the frame.
- **Background:** plain and uncluttered — a wall, foliage well behind you, open sky. Nothing growing out of anyone's head.
- **Light:** face the light, do not stand with your back to it. Open shade on a bright day is the easy answer.
- **Expression:** relaxed and looking at the camera. Not a formal school portrait, not a candid.
- **Shoot both the same day, in the same place, with the same light.** This is the part people get wrong — two portraits in different light look like they came from different websites.
- **Avoid:** sunglasses, harsh top-down sun, heavy filters, cropping too tight.

## What NOT to photograph — ever

This list is binding, not stylistic. See [`COMPLIANCE.md`](COMPLIANCE.md) Rule 3.

- **Any mother or recipient.** Not their face, not from behind, not a hand, not a silhouette, not anonymised.
- **Any bundle being handed over**, or anything that reads as a delivery to a person.
- **The inside of the clinic**, its staff, its signage, or its patients.
- **Any identifiable child**, unless a parent has agreed in writing.
- **Any volunteer who has not agreed** to appear on a public website. For anyone under 18 that means their parent has agreed.

If you are unsure whether someone is identifiable: they are. Frame the shot
around hands, tools, and vegetables instead — it is usually the better photo
anyway.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Golden hour or overcast
- [ ] Lens wiped
- [ ] Ten frames per shot, minimum
- [ ] Shot at plant height, not standing
- [ ] Frame cleared of hoses, bags, pots
- [ ] `hero.jpg` has empty space on the left for the headline
- [ ] Both portraits taken in the same light, same day
- [ ] No mother, no recipient, no handover, no clinic interior
